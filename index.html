⛏️ How a slot actually works — energy, cooling, heat and cost

Cost of one batch

Start from the item's listed price, add the slot upkeep, then the colony's tax. Mining triples the base before any of that, which is already baked into the material prices shown elsewhere in the app.

  • Energy runs 1–21 and cooling 0–21. Energy can't be switched off; cooling can. Most production sits at 5 energy, 0 cooling.
  • Both raise the price, by a flat amount per level: 1.43 UC per energy level and 0.95 per cooling level, so a maxed slot adds 50 UC whether the run costs 69 or 4997.
  • Tax is not a plain markup. A rate above 10% discounts the base first, so 50% works out near 1.33× and even 100% only reaches about 1.50×.

Confirmed against a chemical substance (base 36) on an untaxed colony: 5 energy → 43 UC, 11 energy → 52, and maxed energy and cooling → 86. That last one is what fixes the top of the scale at 21 rather than 22: level 21 contributes exactly the 30 + 20 that makes 86, where level 22 would give 88.

Heat and speed

Energy generates heat continuously and cooling removes it, so the two are in a race rather than at a fixed setting. Heat fluctuates with the energy level and climbs as the run goes on: a pairing that looks balanced early can drift hot once cooling stops keeping up. As heat rises effectiveness falls, and production slows with it.

Read effectiveness off the bar, not the 100% printed beside it — that is the top of the scale, the same as the 100% marks on the energy and cooling columns.

So cooling's 20 UC is not dead weight; it buys sustained speed. And the drift runs both ways: the client lowers the price as heat accumulates, meaning a long hot run is slower but cheaper per unit.

The price drift is now modelled. A plastic run at Paris on 5 bars read 411, 407, 406, 405 … down to 370 over 31 items, and every one of those is reproduced exactly by the arithmetic in calc.txt. So a run's cost is not unit price × count: quoting it that way overstates a 100-unit run by about 14% and a 200-unit run by nearly 30%. Every total in this app now sums the real per-item price across the run, treating each production step as its own session starting cold.

Still not modelled: the speed loss. Heat and effectiveness have no known numbers, so nothing here estimates how long a run takes — only what it costs. Runs longer than about 360 items are also extrapolation; the drift is floored at zero rather than allowed to go negative.
